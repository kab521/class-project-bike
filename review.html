Kawasaki Ninja 300

  • Performance: ★★★★☆
  • Comfort: ★★★★☆
  • Features: ★★☆☆☆
  • Beginners: ★★★★★

    • Best For

  • New riders
  • Riders who commute
  • Smooth rides over power

    Not Ideal For

  • People who value speed
  • People who value the new tech


    • My freshman year at the University of Pittsburgh, I remember seeing this bike parked outside my dorm when I was walking back from class. I watched the girl ride off with it, and I remember thinking that that was the coolest thing ever, since I always thought motorcycles were the stereotypical ugly Harley Davidson cruisers. But I knew I wanted to learn more about it and eventually save up for one. I remembered the name, and as I looked it up, I found out that this bike is actually a sports bike which is meant for speed rather than comfort. It also is actually one of the most common recommendations for beginner riders as well. And I thought, "how lucky is that?" I thought I would share my casual research about the bike in this review and frame it in the way I learned about it.
    The Kawasaki Ninja 300 is a lightweight sport bike that has a strong balance between performance, comfort, and beginner-friendly handling. It has a 296cc twin cylinder engine that produces around 39 horsepower. CCs stand for cubic centimeters, which is the size of the engine's lungs essentially. The higher the CC, the larger the engine, and the more power it has (usually, since it also depends on how the engine is built and how heavy the bike is). The twin cylinder is the engine's configuration, and this one is compact, lightweight, and can reach high speeds. The higher the horse power means that it has a better acceleration, higher top speed, and better performance. The bike is stable, which will help new riders build confidence, while still being new and fun enough for more experienced riders, especially when pushed at higher revs. Despite the sporty ergonomics of it, it still can be super comfortable for daily commuting and long rides, since it has a relaxed seating position and good wind protection.
    However, since it was introduced in 2013, it can have a bit of outdated features such as a basic instrument cluster. That means that the screens located behind the steering wheel that displays all of the vital vehicle data such as speed, fuel level, engine temperature, and warning lights are all kind of generic. In addition, the braking performance could be stronger under aggressive riding. It also can be a bit expensive for what it offers today, at $5,649. That is why a lot of riders recommend buying your first bike second hand for way cheaper, and it can range around $1500. It also offers a safer option in case you crash or damage your bike by accident. Overall, despite the minor negatives, its reliability, smooth engine, and versatility make it a great choice for beginner riders looking for a dependable, easy-to-ride sport bike.


    Shoei X-Fifteen Cross Logo



    The Shoei X-Fifteen (X-15) Cross Logo helmet ranges at $1,099.99 and holds major safety certifications including Snell M2025R, DOT FMVSS 218, and ECE R22/06. It is it is a full-face helmet built for riders who prioritize performance and safety. It was designed using wind tunnel testing and using data from MotoGP statistics to create it. Therefore, it has really great aerodynamic stability, especially at high speeds. This is because it can be resistant to buffeting or lift. This also makes it very great for anyone who wants to ride a sports bike since those bikes usually have riders spending a lot of time in a tucked riding position. It may feel less natural for anyone who is riding on an upright bikes. The best part of the helmet is the fact that it has amazing ventilation, and it is one of its strongest features. This is die to the fact that it having an advanced system of intake and exhaust vents that have great airflow, helping keep the rider cool even during intense or hot-weather rides.

    This is honestly a bit rare for a helmet to have all safety certifications. The DOT certification just consists of mainly drop tests, as it is meant to measure the impact, penetration, and retention testing. A lot of helmets are DOT certified. A Snell certification means that the racing helmet has passed rigorous, independent safety testing is a lot more impressive as it measures high-impact resistance for racing and track use. It is also a voluntary standard, since it has way more strict guidelines than government-mandated DOT requirements. This is due to it featuring comprehensive tests for impacts, penetration, and chin bar strength. Lastly, it also has a ECE certification. This ECE Certification stands for the Economic Commission for Europe. This is to test to see if motorcycle helmets can follow the set of mandatory international safety standards required in over 50 countries, including all of Europe, for road use. So it is very common in Europe helmets, but it is widely considered superior to the US DOT standard because it requires independent, third-party testing of every batch of helmets before they are sold, whereas DOT just allows manufacturers to self-certify.

    Aesthetically, the Cross Logo design gives the helmet a bold, high-end look with sharp graphics and a premium finish that stands out compared to more basic colorways. However, all of this performance and design comes at a premium price, often exceeding $1,000, which may not appeal to casual riders or those on a budget. Additionally, while the helmet performs well in terms of noise for its category, it is not the quietest option available, particularly for long-distance touring. Overall, the Shoei X-Fifteen Cross Logo is an outstanding choice for serious riders and track enthusiasts who want cutting-edge performance and protection, but it may be more helmet than necessary for everyday commuting or relaxed riding styles.
