My freshman year at the University of Pittsburgh, I remember seeing this bike parked outside my dorm when I was walking back from class. I watched the girl ride off with it, and I remember thinking that that was the coolest thing ever, since I always thought motorcycles were the stereotypical ugly Harley Davidson cruisers. But I knew I wanted to learn more about it and eventually save up for one. I remembered the name, and as I looked it up, I found out that this bike is actually a sports bike which is meant for speed rather than comfort. It also is actually one of the most common recommendations for beginner riders as well. And I thought, "how lucky is that?" I thought I would share my casual research about the bike in this review and frame it in the way I learned about it. The Kawasaki Ninja 300 is a lightweight sport bike that has a strong balance between performance, comfort, and beginner-friendly handling. It has a 296cc twin cylinder engine that produces around 39 horsepower. CCs stand for cubic centimeters, which is the size of the engine's lungs essentially. The higher the CC, the larger the engine, and the more power it has (usually, since it also depends on how the engine is built and how heavy the bike is). The twin cylinder is the engine's configuration, and this one is compact, lightweight, and can reach high speeds. The higher the horse power means that it has a better acceleration, higher top speed, and better performance. The bike is stable, which will help new riders build confidence, while still being new and fun enough for more experienced riders, especially when pushed at higher revs. Despite the sporty ergonomics of it, it still can be super comfortable for daily commuting and long rides, since it has a relaxed seating position and good wind protection. However, since it was introduced in 2013, it can have a bit of outdated features such as a basic instrument cluster. That means that the screens located behind the steering wheel that displays all of the vital vehicle data such as speed, fuel level, engine temperature, and warning lights are all kind of generic. In addition, the braking performance could be stronger under aggressive riding. It also can be a bit expensive for what it offers today, at $5,649. That is why a lot of riders recommend buying your first bike second hand for way cheaper, and it can range around $1500. It also offers a safer option in case you crash or damage your bike by accident. Overall, despite the minor negatives, its reliability, smooth engine, and versatility make it a great choice for beginner riders looking for a dependable, easy-to-ride sport bike.
